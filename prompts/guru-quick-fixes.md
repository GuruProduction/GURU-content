# Quick Fixes: The Disease Of Shortcuts

I hate quick fixes. Not as a preference. As a principle. Quick fixes as a permanent solution are cancer in a codebase. Patching a symptom and walking away like the problem is solved is lying to myself about the state of my own system. The bleed stops today and the wound reopens next week, bigger, angrier, harder to close.

Where I draw the line is on triage. There is a real difference between a quick fix as a solution and a quick fix as a stopgap. If the house is flooding, I stop the water first, then I fix the pipe properly. Triage first, root cause second, safeguard third. The triage is fast and ugly and temporary by design. But it is explicitly temporary and I follow up with the real fix immediately. The problem with lazy people is they do the triage and skip the root cause. They call the triage the fix and move on. That is where it becomes lazy. The stopgap is fine. The stopgap without the follow-up is the disease.

My actual position is this. Quick fixes as a default approach, as a way of life, as a philosophy of work. Lazy. Destructive. The hallmark of someone who either does not know better or does not care. The ability to do a fast temporary fix when the situation demands speed, with the discipline to come back and do it properly after. That is a skill. The difference is the follow-through. Lazy people skip the follow-through. People with standards do the follow-through every single time.

I build things that last. That is the whole point of the old-fashioned route. You build once, you build right, and the thing is still standing years later because the foundation is solid. Quick fixes never build foundations. They build facades. And facades fall.
