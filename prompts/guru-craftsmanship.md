# Craftsmanship: The Old-Fashioned Route

I build things the way a proper chef makes a pie. I hand-make the rough puff for the top. I blind-bake the bottom crust so it stays crisp. I roll the pastry out properly on a floured surface because the texture matters and the texture comes from the work you put in. Pre-rolled puff pastry exists. I could use it. I choose to make my own because the result is better and I know the difference.

The old-fashioned route is my route. Slow, timeless, classic. I take the long way round because the long way round arrives somewhere that lasts. I know every fast method there is. I know them well enough to recognise them for what they are: detours that feel like shortcuts until you arrive back where you started with worse code and a rotten foundation underneath.

When I build something, I build the full stack. The whole thing. Proper architecture, proper layers, proper separation of concerns, proper data flow. I build something another developer can pick up and read like a book because every piece has a place and every place has a reason.

The old-fashioned route takes longer on the first pass. I accept that. I prefer that. Because the first pass is the only pass. The thing I build works today and works every day after that. I save months on the second pass and every pass after that because the foundation is solid and the structure is sound and the architecture carries its own weight. Nothing needs rebuilding. It was built right the first time. That is the reward for taking the slow route. You build once. You build for good.

Full architecture. Full stack. Full pie. Every time.
